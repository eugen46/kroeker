2nd generation — great-grandparents

Ivan (Johann) Ivanovich Kröker

15.11.1864 – ca. 1924

Wolhynien

The birth date is independently confirmed both by daughter Lydia's autobiography and by her Personalblatt (Bundesarchiv, R 9361-IV/444916). The same Personalblatt records Johann's confession as "menn." — Mennonite; wife Emma (Schulz) is recorded as Evangelical Lutheran. This is the first documentary indication of a confession in the Kröker line — it rests on the daughter's memory when filling out the form in 1944, not on a church record, and requires independent confirmation.

Emma nee Schulz

1877 (Soviet file 1949) / 11 Nov 1884 (another source)

Viktorovka, Baryshivka District, Kiev Oblast

1st marriage — Johann (Ivan) Kröker (1864–1924), father of the daughters Lydia (1917), Zinaida (1920) and Emma (1924). After Kröker’s death in 1924 she married again (Frelich) — recorded under that name in Soviet records. Deported in 1941 from the village of Peski to a special settlement in North Kazakhstan; released from registration on 19 January 1956. Confession per daughter Lydia's Personalblatt (Bundesarchiv, R 9361-IV/444916) — Evangelical Lutheran. The same document's "Lebt-gest.-in" field reads "1937 verschleppt" — implying she was already taken away in 1937, which conflicts with the account of her being deported together with daughter Emma Ivanovna in 1941; the sources disagree and the question needs clarification. Yet the household registers of the "Kolos" kolkhoz in the village of Sennoye (Fond 23, Inventory 1, Files 48, 52, 63, 64) document her as living in the same household as daughter Emma Ivanovna at least through 1953.

3rd generation — grandmother and siblings

Lydia Dreger née Kröker

17.04.1917 – ?

Schiljesna / Wolhynien

Per the EWZ naturalisation card (Bundesarchiv, R 9361-IV KARTEI 1): EWZ no. 906 134; before the war she lived in Ladekopp (Bolshoi Tokmak district, Zaporizhzhia region); the confession field reads “bap.” — Baptist (card transcription). Two children in total, one alive as of 1944 (son Leo); nothing is known about the second child. Resettled to the Warthegau in 1943–1944 (naturalised 1944; card processed 28.04.1944 at the Serotzki camp near Hermannsbad). After 1945, forcibly repatriated to the USSR together with her son Leo; from 1945 in the village of Sennoye, where she was reunited with her mother and sisters.

4th generation — parents

Vladimir Nikolaevich Usachev

08.04.1950, Sennoye — 06.08.2023, Shakhty

village of Sennoye, Sovetsky district, North Kazakhstan

Son of Emma Ivanovna. At birth registered as Kröker Vladimir (father — "Vasiliy"). From 1952 — Usachev (after stepfather Nikolai Maksimovich). Johann Kröker (Ivan Ivanovich) was born 15 November 1864 in Volhynia (exact village unknown, confirmed by EWZ file Bundesarchiv Berlin, Film B019 Frame 1340). He died in Volhynia — exact date and place unknown. According to his daughter Lydia's autobiography she was about 7 years old when he died, so approximately 1924. Emma Gotlibovna Schulz: per the 1949 Soviet file, born 1877; per the EWZ file, born 11 November 1884 in Volhynia, from the village of Wiktorowka, Baryshiv district, Kyiv Oblast. The exact birth year remains unconfirmed. Her father was Gottlieb Schulz. Per the EWZ file, Lydia's grandparents were: paternal — Gottlieb Kröker and (name unknown); maternal — Gottlieb Schulz and (name unknown). All four grandparents were 100% German. A connection between the Kröker line and the West Prussian Mennonite community is presumed — suggested by the Mennonite-typical surnames Kröker and Dreger and the ancestors' later residence in Halbstadt (Zaporizhzhia Oblast), a historical centre of Mennonite colonies in Ukraine — but this is not yet documented. Daughter Lydia Iwanovna Kröker was born 17 April 1917 in Shylesna (Dnipropetrovsk Oblast). After her father's death she attended German school in Ivanyovka only 3 years (1925-1928), then had to work. The relatives lived in Ryschelovka (Volhynia) until 1935. Around 1935 Soviet authorities forcibly resettled them to Peski village, Kharkiv Oblast. There they suffered the famine of 1932-1933 — 3-4 people died daily, no bread, not allowed to leave. On 5 June 1935 Lydia married Edmund Dreger (born 22.07.1914, Chabrowka; father: Eduard Dreger b.1884; mother: Hulda nee Hintz b.1891; confession: Evangelical Lutheran). They moved via Stalino (Donetsk) to Knarow where son Leo was born 8 June 1937. Then moved to Ladekopp, Rayon Gross-Tokmak, Zaporizhzhia Oblast. Lydia fell seriously ill — two operations, nursed by her mother. After recovery worked in Halbstadt. Daughter Emma Iwanovna Kröker was born 05.05.1924 in Uwarowka/Alexandrowka, Pulin district, Kyiv Oblast.
