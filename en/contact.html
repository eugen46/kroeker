Questions and Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Which surnames does the archive cover?

Kröker (Kroeker, Крёкер), Schulz (Шульц), Dreger (Дрегер), Fröhlich (Frelich, Фрёлих), Usachew (Усачёв).

Where did the Krökers come from?

Mennonites from West Prussia (Danzig / Gdańsk), resettled to Volhynia in the 1860s.

What happened to the relatives in 1941?

Deportation to Northern Kazakhstan, the village of Sennoje — together with other Volhynian Germans.

Where did the relatives live after Kazakhstan?

From 1955 — Schachty (Rostov region); from 2019 — Berlin.

What documents are in the archive?

Archival certificates from the Kazakhstan Prosecutor's Office, Kharkiv State Archive, MWD; EWZ50 Film B019 Bundesarchiv Berlin (frames 1340–1364).

I found my surname in your archive — what should I do?

Write through the contact page or Telegram. You can send a document or request an excerpt.

May I use the materials from this website?

All materials are copyrighted. For use — please contact the author.

Is this a commercial project?

No, a private non-commercial research project. All donations go solely to archive certificates and trips.

What is EWZ?

Einwandererzentralstelle — Nazi-era central card index of ethnic Germans, 1939–1945. Archive in Berlin, source of biographical data on the Krökers.

How do I search for relatives via DNA?

Tests with MyHeritage / Ancestry / 23andMe + uploading raw data to GEDmatch.com (free match search).
