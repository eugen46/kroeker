EWZ naturalisation card: Lydia Dreger née Kröker

Document description

Archive: Bundesarchiv, Berlin-Lichterfelde.
Collection / series: R 9361-IV KARTEI 1 — EWZ-Einbürgerungskartei (Einwandererzentralstelle naturalisation card index).
Person: DREGER, née Kröcker, Lydia.
EWZ no.: 906 134.
Processing date: 28 April 1944.
Extent: two cards — typed and handwritten (duplicate of the same data), each with a reverse side.

Scans of the cards are not published on this site: obtaining a copy from an archive is not in itself permission to publish it online. Below is a field-by-field transcription of the data.

What the document confirms and what it refines

Confirms: Lydia’s date and place of birth (17.04.1917, Shilesna), the year of marriage (1935), the birth of her son Leo in 1937, the birth year of her husband Edmund Dreger (1914) and his disappearance in 1941, the pre-war residence at Ladekopp (Bolshoi Tokmak district), and the date 28 April 1944 — the day Lydia herself names in her autobiography as the day she applied for German citizenship.

New information: individual resettlement numbers for Lydia and her son Leo (not published — Leo, b. 1937, could theoretically still be alive); the entry “2 children, of which 1 living” — Lydia therefore had another child who did not survive those years (name and dates not established); the Serotzki camp near Hermannsbad as the place of registration.

Discrepancies between sources: the district of birth is recorded as Artemovsk, whereas Lydia’s autobiography places Shilesna in the Dnipropetrovsk region. The card gives two classes of schooling, the autobiography “only three years”. Which is correct cannot be determined from the documents at hand.

On the “Konfession (Sekte)” field: both cards carry the handwritten entry “bap.” — Baptist. This is the first documentary indication of a confession in this line. It refers to Lydia personally in 1944 and neither confirms nor refutes the presumption that the Kröker name is connected with the West Prussian Mennonite community: confession could change across generations, and German registration records of the period frequently used this entry for congregations close to the Baptists. The question requires confirmation from church registers.

Personalblatt (R 9361-IV/444916)

In addition to the card, the Bundesarchiv sent a scan of Lydia Dreger\'s Personalblatt (personal data sheet), with the same EWZ file number 906 134. A photo of Lydia from around 1944 is glued to the back — it is not published here, per the site\'s general policy ("Sources and Licenses").

New information from the Personalblatt:

  • Lydia\'s parents: father — Johann Kröker, born 15.11.1864, place unknown, confession "menn." (Mennonite), died 1924 in Volhynia; mother — Emma, née Schulz, born 11.11.1884 in Volhynia, Evangelical Lutheran. The field for the mother also reads "1937 verschleppt" — implying she was already taken away in 1937, which conflicts with the already-published account of her deportation together with daughter Emma Ivanovna in 1941; the discrepancy is unresolved (see also Emma Schulz\'s card on the "Family Tree" page).
  • Parents of Edmund Dreger (Lydia\'s husband): father — Eduard Dreger, b. 1884, Chabrowka, Evangelical Lutheran, taken away by Soviet authorities in 1941; mother — Hulda, née Hintz, b. 1891, Chabrowka, same confession.
  • Son Leo: birthplace refined — Knorow, Nikolayevka district (previously on the site, per Lydia\'s autobiography, "Knarow").
  • Place of registration — Seroczki, Koneck municipality, Hermannsbad district, "near Bugantka"; border crossing on 28 April 1944 — matches the card\'s data.

Household registers of the "Kolos" kolkhoz, village of Sennoye

Separately obtained excerpts from the household registers of the Sennoye village council for 1948–1953 (Fond 23, Inventory 1, Files 48, 52, 63, 64) independently confirm the family reunion in Sennoye already described on the site:

  • In the same household as Emma Ivanovna Kroeker are recorded her mother Emma (Frelich), b. 1877, her sisters Lydia Dreger (1917) with son Leo (1937), and Zinaida Simentsova (1920) — all together, with German nationality noted.
  • Emma Gotlibovna (Frelich) is documented as living in this household at least through 1953 — see the note on the discrepancy with the "1937 verschleppt" entry above.
  • From 1945, Lydia Dreger is also recorded as head of a separate household in the same kolkhoz; her son Leo is noted as attending school in 1951–1953.
