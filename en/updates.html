28 July 2026 — Personalblatt and household registers

In addition to the card, the Bundesarchiv sent a scan of Lydia Dreger’s Personalblatt (R 9361-IV/444916) with a photo on the back — the photo is not published. New: Lydia’s parents — Johann Kröker (b. 15.11.1864, confession “menn.” — Mennonite) and Emma, née Schulz (b. 11.11.1884, Evangelical Lutheran); this is the first documentary indication of a Mennonite confession specifically in the Kröker line, though it rests on the daughter’s memory rather than a church record. The entry for mother Emma also reads “1937 verschleppt”, which conflicts with the already-published account of her deportation together with daughter Emma Ivanovna in 1941 — the discrepancy is unresolved. Parents of Edmund Dreger — Eduard (b. 1884) and Hulda, née Hintz (b. 1891). Leo’s birthplace refined — Knorow.

Separately obtained excerpts from the household registers of the “Kolos” kolkhoz, village of Sennoye, 1948–1953 (Akkayin District Archive, Fond 23, Inventory 1, Files 48, 52, 63, 64) confirm: Emma Gotlibovna (Frelich) is documented as living in the same household as daughter Emma Ivanovna at least through 1953; Lydia Dreger is head of her own household in the same kolkhoz from 1945, son Leo attended school in 1951–1953. Also found: a previously unmentioned daughter of Emma Ivanovna — Lyudmila, b. 1952 (see the “Family Tree” page). Details and transcription →

27 July 2026 — Bundesarchiv

Reply from the Bundesarchiv (Berlin-Lichterfelde) on request 2026/KW08#0359#0002: file R 9361-IV/18702 is available for consultation in the reading room from 29 July 2026. Separately, a scan of the EWZ naturalisation card for Lydia Dreger née Kröker was sent (R 9361-IV KARTEI 1). From the card: EWZ no. 906 134; last home place Ladekopp, Bolshoi Tokmak district, Zaporizhzhia region; husband Edmund Dreger, born 1914, marked “taken away in 1941”; registered by a commission at the Serotzki camp near Hermannsbad, processed 28 April 1944 — the very day Lydia names in her autobiography as the day she applied for citizenship. Two new details: the entry “2 children, of which 1 living” — so there was another child about whom nothing is known; and the “Konfession (Sekte)” field reading “bap.” — Baptist. This is the first documentary indication of a confession in this line; it neither confirms nor refutes the presumption of Mennonite roots and requires verification against church registers. The archive also reported that there is no photograph either in the file or on the card; the Personalblattsammlung has additionally been ordered — if a photo is found there, it will be sent as a scan. 18 July 2026 — Online sources

New leads for the Volhynian lines found in open databases. The expropriation list of German land in Pulin volost (Volhynian Governorate Gazette, 02.06.1916) names Dreger Christian August (12 dessiatins) — documentary evidence of the Dreger line in Pulin volost. The 1913 register of landowners of Volhynia Governorate (Novograd-Volynsk uyezd) lists Schulz Pawel (54 dess.) and Schulz Julius (85 dess.). According to GAMEO, the Kroeker surname was first recorded in Tiegenhagen (West Prussia) in 1627; the Mennonite colony of Karolswalde in Volhynia emigrated almost entirely to Kansas in 1874–1875, so the Kroekers of the Pulin district most likely belonged to the Lutheran Volhynian Germans. For the uncles Heinrich (Bogoslovlag) and Friedrich (Bakalstroy) Schulz the "Electronic Memorial Book of Russian Germans" (gedenkbuch.rusdeutsch.ru) was found — checking continues.
